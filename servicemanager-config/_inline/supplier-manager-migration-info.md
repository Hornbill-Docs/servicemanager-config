# Supplier Management is moving to Service Manager

Supplier management is becoming a native part of Service Manager. Over the coming releases, your suppliers and contracts will move out of the standalone Supplier Manager app and into Service Manager alongside the rest of your service management data.

## Why we are making this change

Supplier Manager was designed as a general-purpose application for recording an organization's suppliers and contracts. It was never designed to underpin the specific needs of IT supplier management workflows, and as a result sits apart from Service Manager, with only arm's length integration points between the two.

In practice that means today it is difficult to answer questions like:

* Which assets are covered by this support contract, and when does that cover expire?
* Which supplier underpins this service, and what are we entitled to when something goes wrong?
* Which contracts are up for renewal, and what depends on them?

Building on the work we have done with Assets, moving supplier management directly into Service Manager means suppliers, contracts, assets and services all live in the same place and can be better integrated. That integration is the whole purpose of the change - it is what allows supplier information to be more closely tied to asset information and become genuinely useful in IT service management workflows.

Supplier management inside Service Manager can be developed specifically around IT supplier management workflows and needs - the renewals, the entitlements, the asset cover, the supplier performance questions that IT teams actually deal with. That is a direction the standalone app was never going to be able to take us in.

## What you will get

The first release is a like-for-like move. Everything you do in Supplier Manager today you will be able to do in Service Manager under the "Suppliers" area:

* Create, view, update and delete supplier records.
* Record and manage contracts against each supplier.
* View contracts across all of your suppliers, with sorting and filtering - including by expiry date - so renewals are easy to keep on top of.
* Associate assets with a contract, and see the full list of covered assets from within the contract itself.

We have deliberately kept this first step focused on matching what you have today, so the move is as low-risk as possible. Nothing you rely on is being left behind. Once that foundation is in place, the improvements can follow.

## What this means for the Supplier Manager app

From everything we can see, our customers use Supplier Manager for one purpose: managing their IT suppliers. If that describes you, then once your data has moved you will have no reason to keep using the standalone app - Service Manager will do everything you were using it for, in the place where the rest of your service management work already happens.

That is why we are treating this as a move rather than as a second option to maintain. Supplier Manager will be retired once customers have transitioned.

If you use Supplier Manager for something beyond IT supplier management, we would genuinely like to know. Please get in touch so we can understand your use case before the retirement takes effect.

## What happens to your existing data

Your data comes with you. When the migration runs, your existing supplier records, contracts and asset associations are copied across into Service Manager.

* You do not need to re-enter anything.
* You do not need to export or back up your data first.
* Your existing records keep their details and relationships.

## What you need to do now

Nothing. There is no action required from you today, and Supplier Manager continues to work exactly as it does now.

Supplier Manager and the new capability in Service Manager will run alongside each other during the transition, giving you time to become familiar with the new experience before anything is switched off. We will give you clear notice, in advance, before Supplier Manager is retired.

## Questions
